01 — PALETTE
Kill the gradient. Keep one color, and make it the sport's.
Your read is right: navy + blue→green gradient is the uniform of AI dev tools, and gradients read as marketing, not measurement. The fix isn't a different gradient — it's monochrome. Near-black neutrals carry the whole interface (the Workouts-app register), and a single accent does all the pointing. That accent is optic yellow-green — the color of the ball itself. It's the one hue that says "this sport" without drawing a paddle, and against true neutrals it reads as instrument signal, not decoration. Rule: the accent only ever marks the thing that matters right now — one CTA, one data line, one selected state. Never backgrounds, never headings.
02 — TYPE
Three voices: verdict, coach, instrument.
Archivo (heavy, expanded for the wordmark, tabular for numbers) delivers verdicts and metrics — it has the flat-sided authority of stadium signage without being a sports cliché. Instrument Sans is the coach's speaking voice: body copy, explanations, buttons. IBM Plex Mono is the instrument itself — every label, unit, and countdown is mono smallcaps, which is what makes the UI feel calibrated rather than styled. No font is ever italic, and nothing is ever centered except the reveal.
03 — LOGO
The mark is the plateau, broken.
No paddle, no ball, no net. The mark is two strokes: a flat gray line — the plateau your user has been living on — and an accent line breaking upward off its end. It's the product's promise drawn in four points, it's legible at 16px, and it doubles as a chart motif across the app (the Progress chart is literally the logo happening slowly). The wordmark is expanded-width Archivo caps: "ROAD TO NEXT" carries the weight, "RATING" sits below in mono as the measured object.
